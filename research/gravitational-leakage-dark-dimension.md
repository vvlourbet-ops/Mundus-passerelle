# Gravitational leakage: bridging a dark dimension and gravitational-wave propagation

**Status:** Open research question / conceptual comparison, not a result or a claim of discovery.  
**Date:** 12 November 2026  
**Workspace:** Mundus-passerelle

## Question

Can the gravitational degrees of freedom associated with the compact extra dimension proposed in the *dark dimension* programme be related, in any controlled limit, to the anomalous gravitational-wave amplitude damping studied in higher-dimensional braneworld phenomenology? If not, which physical scales or observables make the two approaches distinguishable?

## Two distinct starting points

1. **Dark dimension (Vafa and collaborators).** A compact extra spatial dimension, with a tower of Kaluza–Klein (KK) gravitational states. Depending on the model, these states may contribute to dark-matter phenomenology. The compactification radius sets KK mass gaps, schematically `m_n c^2 ~ n ħc/R` (up to geometric factors). A compact dimension is not automatically an open channel for continuous radiative energy loss.
2. **Gravitational-wave leakage (Chen & Zhang, 2026).** A phenomenological modification of gravitational-wave luminosity distance, motivated by higher-dimensional propagation at cosmological scales:

   `d_L^GW / d_L^EM = [1 + (d_L^EM / (R_c (1+z)))^n]^((D-4)/(2n))`

   Here `D` is an effective spacetime dimensionality, `R_c` a crossover distance, and `n` a transition-shape parameter. The analysis fixes `n=1`, assumes a ΛCDM background and constrains propagation using 141 GWTC-4 compact-binary events and galaxy information.

**Do not identify the compactification radius `R` with the cosmological crossover scale `R_c`.** They represent different physics and may differ by many orders of magnitude.

## What the observations currently say

For the authors' narrow `H_0` prior and `log10(R_c/Mpc) ∈ [2.7, 4.0]`, Chen & Zhang report `D = 4.38 (-1.01, +1.91)` at 68% credibility. Four-dimensional general relativity is consistent with the observations. The posterior for `R_c` piles up near its upper prior bound; allowing larger `R_c` substantially weakens the constraint on `D`. This is **not evidence of leakage** or detection of a fifth dimension.

## Proposed theoretical checks

1. **Specify an explicit 5D action and geometry.** Choose a concrete compactification and brane/bulk coupling; do not combine equations from incompatible models without derivation.
2. **Linearize and KK-decompose.** Obtain the spectrum, mode wavefunctions and their coupling to brane-localized sources.
3. **Check propagation thresholds.** For a compact dimension, determine whether detector-band gravitational waves can excite propagating massive KK modes. Below threshold, look for virtual corrections or dispersion rather than assuming an energy-loss channel.
4. **Derive a brane-to-brane Green function.** Compute amplitude and phase versus frequency, redshift and propagation distance, accounting for cosmological expansion where relevant.
5. **Compare observables, not just vocabulary.** Test whether the derived waveform predicts a distance-only damping law, a frequency-dependent distortion, an additional polarization, or no detectable correction. Only then compare with the Chen–Zhang `d_L^GW/d_L^EM` parameterization.
6. **Check independent constraints.** Laboratory tests of gravity, cosmology, astrophysics and gravitational-wave data may rule out parameter regions before any proposed fit.
7. **State falsifiers.** Identify parameter combinations and observational signatures that would distinguish compact KK physics from large-distance leakage and ordinary 4D general relativity.

## Key conceptual distinction

A fifth dimension can be present without producing the specific cosmological damping law tested by Chen & Zhang. Conversely, anomalous GW damping need not imply Vafa's dark-dimension model. A meaningful bridge requires a derived mapping between an underlying theory and a measurable waveform.
